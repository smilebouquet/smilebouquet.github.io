We're all alone (Song Translation)

Lyrics: Matsui Youhei
Composition: Joshua Leung & Kurihara Satoru (Jazzin’park)
Arrangement: Joshua Leung

We're all alone…

You say you believe ignorance is bliss
But aren't you just locking yourself away in your tranquil heart? (Where you are)1

Rather than letting you stay in a stagnant place where even the wind won't blow
I'll accept your existence as you are—your suffering... your joy... your sins

We're all alone, but that's why we step into the world
If you can feel the wind, surely you'll hear my words, too
Even if human connections are difficult for you
Do not be afraid, for your knowledge is where everything in this world begins

I will continue to gather the sands of truth2
Until it shapes your unfulfilled heart (What you want)

Even if coveting love as recurrent as the waves is a sin
I have made my choice... I shall walk alongside your existence

We're all alone, but that's why we long to connect with others
Perhaps now you'll grasp the significance behind my wind-carried song

Getting everything you want is no different from never feeling loss
Aah, but doesn't light exist to shine upon humans in the dark?

We're all alone, but that's why we step into the world
If you can hear my song, surely my thoughts will reach you, too
If a miracle occurred, if someone were to return your gaze
And it lasted an eternity, surely this world will expand beautifully... Evermore

  1. 波立つ (namidatsu) means "to be choppy (of waves); to ripple" or "to be in turmoil", so 波⽴たない (namidatanai), which is what's used in the song proper, would naturally mean the opposite. fittingly, the first character in nagisa's given name (凪) means "calm (at sea)" or "lull". i chose to translate this into 'tranquil', which is a word i've seen be used to describe calm seas and can be used to mean "free from storms" (according to merriam webster my beloved).
  2. the 砂 kanji from 砂粒 (subatsubu) means "sand". it's also the second character in nagisa's given name.
