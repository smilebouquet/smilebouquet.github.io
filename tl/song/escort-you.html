  1. the personal pronoun 私 is used here. this is the pronoun tsukasa uses in stories, voice lines, etc.

    for reference, he uses 自分 in his first solo (with my honesty), and i couldn’t find any personal pronouns in his second solo (shall we dance?).
  2. the second-person pronoun used here is あなた (anata); he used 貴女 (also anata but female-leaning) in his first two solos.

    it’s important to note that, just like how he uses his actual personal pronoun in this song, あなた is the second-person pronoun he uses in stories, voice lines, etc.
  3. 群青色 (gunjou’iro), or simply 群青 (gunjou), is commonly translated as “ultramarine blue” but specifically refers to a deep shade of blue with a slight purple tint. unlike ultramarine blue, which is derived from lapis lazuli, gunjou is derived from azurite. It was highly valued back in the day due to its rarity and used sparingly in buddhist statues and paintings. To this day, it’s still a highly coveted pigment for japanese paintings and the like, similarly to how ultramarine blue is still popular in the modern era. you can read more about the history of the colour gunjou here.

    in literature and poetry, when “gunjou” is used to refer to the sky, they’re evoking the image of a clear, cloudless sky.
  4. 胸に落ちる (mune ni ochiru) is a phrase that means “to understand”, but when read in a literal manner means “to sink in one’s chest”. i couldn’t find an english word or phrase that would convey both meanings, so I settled on ‘enlighten’ to convey the "to help understand" meaning as well as pay homage to the line's sunset imagery.
