  1. as nonsensical as it may sound, "tru-tu-tu" comes from トゥルルル (turururu), which is the sound of a ringing phone.
  2. ツーカー (tsuukaa) is a slang term that broadly means "to operate on the same wavelength". the origins of the word are unclear but it's speculated that it's a shortened form of the phrase つうといえばかあ (tsuu to ieba kaa, "when i say 'tsuu', 'kaa'."), which comes from the idea that if two people are close, when one person says "that's basically it." (つうことだ, tsuu koto da), their partner will immediately say "i see," (そうかあ, sou kaa) without needing more details. it's quite the antiquated phrase—as a testament to this, there used to be a telephone service under the name tu-ka (spelled ツーカー in japanese!) that was in service from 1991-2006. it's very likely the name tu-ka was chosen because of ツーカー.

    i chose to translate this into "pick up what (someone) put down", because the verb 'pick up' is heavily associated with telephones.
  3. 二転三転 (niten santen) is a four character compound word that means "to change again and again".
  4. イチイチ (ichi-ichi) is a word that can mean either "one by one" or "in detail". it can be written as 一一, with 一 being the kanji for the number one, making this word literally "one-one"... aka 11.
