  1. 狂宴 (kyou'en, “wild party”) is homophones with 競演 (also kyou'en, “contest between performers”). this is why the in-game mv depicts health bars above them like they're in a fighting game :)
  2. ダージャーハオ (大家好 dàjiā hǎo) means “hello, everyone” in chinese. it’s a common stage greeting.
  3. this is a callback to a line in kangei☆2wink zatsugidan, a song from 2wink's very first single that was released in 2015. i will be shortening it to 'zatsugidan' henceforth.

    anyway, the line in zatsugidan goes 満員御礼…夢見ていこう！/ "we’ll be dreaming of a full house!"
  4. イーアルサンスー (一二三四 yī èr sān sì) are the first four numbers in chinese.

    additionally, this line is another callback to zatsugidan (リズムに合わせ宙返り / "syncing with the rhythm as we somersault").
  5. モーマンタイ(無問題 mò mun thì) means “no problem”. it’s a cantonese phrase that was popularised in japan by a 1999 hongkong movie with the same name.
  6. 謝謝 (谢谢 xiè xie) means “thank you” in chinese. the twins perform the chinese ‘thank you’ gesture during the choreography. (they also say “xiè xie” in zatsugidan!)
  7. 来来 (lài lai) is from 来, which means “come”. can also be used as an invitation (similarly to “welcome!”).
  8. ウーリューチーパー (五六七八 wǔ liù qī bā) are the next four numbers in chinese. fun fact, the number 8 is considered lucky due to its similar reading to the word for wealth/prosperity (发 fā).
  9. 大波 小波 (oonami konami, "big waves, small waves"), is from a japanese jump rope song where one person swings the rope however they want, and the person jumping has to match their rhythm. in essence, this line is mocking the idea that 2wink’s stunts are 'just' jump rope tricks.
  10. ウォーアイニー (我爱你 wǒ ài nǐ) means “i love you”.
  11. another callback to zatsugidan! the original line is キミのね、笑顔が見たくて / "we wanna see you smile".
